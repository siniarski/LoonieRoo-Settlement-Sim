- **2026-11-05, world generation as a pipeline (branch towns).** Player: "My expectation is the natural world is generated first (forests, water), then towns are placed in realistic areas with roads generated to each other and town roads branching off with enough distance between them. The lots are generated of various zones and sizes to fit (they don't have to be strictly grid), once the lots are placed the buildings are placed inside the lot." Rewrote worldgen to do exactly that; the block grid, districts by block and main roads every 64 tiles are gone.
  - nature.ts: water (sea, channels, the river, lakes) and forest, per tile, as before (split out of worldgen.ts).
  - worldplan.ts: the plan, made once per (seed, size, map) and cached (derived data, never saved; about 15 ms at 256, 100 ms at 512). 1) towns.ts sites towns: a count for the map's size (one or two on 128, two or three on 256, about ten on 512), village (radius 20-28), town (34-46) or city (52-66, maps of 384 and up), capped at a fifth of the map; biggest first, each on the best-scoring spot (dry share 90%+, little forest, water just outside it), whole on the map, clear of the start (radius + 22) and of other towns (radii + 48). 2) Each town grows streets: a main street and cross street from its centre, side streets off them every 26-31 tiles (the first 18-24 out) with some chance each way, and in towns and cities side streets of their own; a street stops at water, the start, the town's edge, where it meets another street, and where it would run within 9 tiles alongside one. 3) roads.ts joins the towns and the start (a minimum spanning tree plus a few short loops, and some roads out to the map's edge; on a quarantine map always one, to a gate): A* over 4-tile cells (land 1, woods 3, water 14 so it bridges only where it must, sea impassable, other towns +4, roads already laid 0.35 so roads share), from street ends; corner-cut into curves, drawn about 2.5 wide. 4) Lots: along both sides of every street (a tile's verge), zoned by the lot's distance from the centre: civic (20-26 by 18-24, main streets, 1 to 4 a town), shops (12-15 by 12-14), houses (12-15 by 13-15), now and then industry at the edge (22-28 by 18-24); a narrower house lot when a big one won't fit. Then houses along the country roads where a town spills out, farms (a farmstead and fields, or fields alone, 26-39 by 22-31) along roads further out, fields ringing each town (facing it, a quarter with farmsteads), and cabins out in the wild. Lots never touch roads, water, each other or the start.
  - lots.ts: each lot's layout is planned in its own frame (along the frontage, back from the street) and turned onto the map: a house set back, maybe a garden and a shed behind; a shop at the street (a petrol station with forecourt and pumps); a civic building on paving with benches; a warehouse on concrete; a farm's house and barn by the road with a silo, bales and a tractor, crops in rows behind, hedged (or an orchard, or pasture). A chunk draws its part of every lot touching it (a lot can span chunks now), so chunks still come out the same in any order.
