- **2026-11-11, textures: the detail kit and the world's surfaces.** The player asked for textures on everything; the world was flat vertex colour. This session made the shared kit (the models' and the living things' sessions build on it) and textured the ground, walls, doors, windows, ceilings and roofs. docs/textures.md is the kit's page.
  - **The kit** (commit d0adc39; the surfaces 963a1c4). `art/scripts/build_surfaces.py` (numpy only, seeded by name) writes twenty tileable 256 px pictures to `godot/assets/surfaces/` and `layers.json`: grass, litter, earth, gravel, asphalt, paving, concrete, plaster, brick, planks, floorboards, tiles, shingles, sheetmetal, metal, rubber, cloth, grime, bark, fur. A texel is a multiplier stored halved and every channel averages 0.5 exactly, so a surface keeps its colour and the far view needs no texture. `godot/assets/shaders/surface_detail.gdshaderinc` lays a layer over a colour by world position (static), object position (movers) or the mesh's UV2 (roof slopes, skinned bodies): one sample a fragment, no branches, faded out from 26 to 48 m. `godot/World3D/Surfaces.cs` loads the set into one Texture2DArray, the global `surface_layers` (a global must exist before a shader naming it compiles: `Surfaces.Shader(code)`).
  - **How a face says its layer: a number on the vertex, not a surface per material.** UV.x is 0 (none), layer + 1 (by position) or -(layer + 1) (by UV2); UV.y is repeats per metre, negative turned a quarter. A chunk stays one surface, one material and one draw, as before; a surface per material would have been up to ten draws a chunk for 49 chunks. Vertex colour's alpha was not free (the models keep metalness there).
  - **What is textured and how it is chosen** (`godot/World3D/WorldLook.cs`; nothing added to the sim):
    - Ground by its kind (Grids.GROUNDS): grass, forest litter, earth (dirt, fields, the river bed), asphalt, paving, floorboards, tiles, concrete, planks on bridges. Water is left flat.
    - Walls by the building: Roofs.cs already knew each lot's buildings from the world plan; it now keeps their style by tile (`Roofs.Styles`), from the plan's kind and style and the lot's roll. Houses: siding in five paints, brick or render; farmhouses siding or brick; cabins and sheds rough boards; barns red or weathered boards; trailers, warehouses, factories, workshops and stations corrugated sheet or block; shops, bars and offices brick or render; police, clinic and school brick or block. A wall's face looks like the inside of the building it faces into (plaster in five off-whites; the back of the boards, block or sheet in barns, sheds, cabins and industrial buildings), else the outside of the one behind it. A wall that is nobody's, and what you build yourself, is bare boards (walls are put up with wood).
    - Doors upright boards; window frames (a sill, a head, two stiles: new geometry, 20 quads a window) and the reveals of doors and windows in the building's trim; grime on glass (paler and less clear where it lies); barricades and boards over windows planks; ceilings plaster, boards or sheet by the building; a concrete footing under a wall on a slope.
    - Roofs: shingles or a barn's sheet by the slope's own coordinates (along the eave, down the slope: level courses whichever way the ridge runs, no stretch from the pitch), tar and gravel on flat roofs, the parapet and the gable in the building's wall, boards on what you roof yourself. Snow whitens roofs now (`snowy()` from snow_cover.gdshaderinc, which nothing used).
    - The far view's blocks wear the same wall and roof colours as the near buildings (FarField.cs asks WorldLook.Of and Roofs.RoofOf), so a brick shop is not grey at 60 m.
  - **The ground has colours of its own in 3D** (a commit of its own, to revert alone: `WorldLook.GroundColour`). The ground used the Colony tab's palette, near-greys made for a dark 2D map, which the sun lit to one pale grey for grass, road and paving alike: no texture can make that read as grass. Now grass is a muted green, forest floor darker, dirt and fields brown, roads dark grey, floors by their material; first person and the far view take the colour from the one function, so they still meet. The Colony tab and the minimap keep the palette. The per-tile lightness steps (0.06 to 0.095) are narrowed to 0.07 to 0.085 near you: under a texture they read as metre squares.
  - **Cost** (experiments/textures/bench.sh: the light bench's 300 frames, 1280 x 720, vsync off, two runs each; this machine's GPU is fast, so read the ratio):

    | place | frame ms before | after | GPU ms before | after |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | street | 2.50, 2.46 | 2.50, 2.62 | 0.35 | 0.37 |
    | inside a house | 2.58, 2.62 | 2.53, 2.66 | 0.36 | 0.37-0.38 |
    | open country | 2.27, 2.26 | 2.39, 2.50 | 0.44 | 0.45-0.46 |
    | looking down at the road | 2.44, 2.50 | 2.54, 2.48 | 0.39 | 0.40 |

    About 0.01-0.02 ms of GPU a frame (3-5%); the frame times are inside their noise. Memory: ~7 MB for the array, 8 bytes a vertex for the UV. If a weak GPU feels it, the first cut is the include's filter (`filter_linear_mipmap_anisotropic` to `filter_linear_mipmap`), which costs the ground at a distance a little sharpness and nothing else.
  - **Pictures** (experiments/textures/, `shots.sh before|after` retakes them): before- and after- street, house, country, snow, night; after-roofs and after-town from above; kit.png, every layer.
  - **Decided.** Layers are numbered: add at the end of LAYERS, never reorder. Detail textures stay neutral (mean 0.5): colour belongs to the vertex, so anything can be recoloured without a new texture. The world's faces go through WorldLook, not constants in WorldMesh.
  - **Left.** Water is flat colour. Indoors is dim (the ambient light under a roof, as before), so plaster's detail hardly shows. The far view's blocks are still flat-roofed boxes in one colour a side. Brick's mortar is pinkish on a red base (a multiplier cannot lighten past double). Rooms are not told apart (a kitchen's walls are the hall's): the sim knows each plan's rooms, the view does not ask yet. Hand-made maps (no world plan) draw every edge wall as bare boards, and tile walls as grey concrete. Shimmer was judged on stills only (mipmaps, anisotropic filtering and the fade are in): the player's eye in motion is the test. Models, people, animals, trees and undergrowth are the next sessions'.
