- **2026-11-10, world lighting.** The player asked for lights in the world: room lights with switches, outdoor house
  lights, street lamps, car lights, a flashlight, the stove and fire, sim first, then the picture. Built
  `cs/Sim/Systems/Lights.cs` (the rules and one query), `godot/World3D/Lights.cs` (real lights within a budget, glows
  beyond), the keys G and X, debug flags for pictures (`godot/GameLights.cs`), and replaced the automatic "indoors is
  lit while the mains are on" (`TimelineMod.mainsLit`, gone) with switches.
  - **The rules as built** (Lights.cs header has them in full).
    - Rooms: every room of a generated building (the plan's rooms, Buildings.toEdges, turned onto the map, cached per
      placement) and every room you wall in (Rooms) has a light behind a switch: lit at night when on and powered (the
      mains, or a fuelled generator within POWER_RADIUS of the room's anchor tile). Default: a quarter of homes (LEFT_ON,
      a hash of the lot and building) have one room left on, the room picked by a hash; every other switch is off.
      A room light lights exactly its room's tiles. A room on a generator loads it at night once the mains are off.
    - The switch: command `lightSwitch {x, y}` (a tile of the room), for the survivor you control in the room or on a
      tile across one of its doors. Only switches that differ from their default are kept.
    - Porch lights by the front door of houses, shops and civic buildings (85% work), street lamps every 12 tiles along
      town streets on the kerb, side and offset by a hash (80% work): at night while the mains are on.
    - Headlights: command `headlights` for the driver; lit while the car has fuel and isn't wrecked. The sim has no
      battery: fuel stands in for it and the lights burn none.
    - Flashlight: a new item kind; `flashlight` command or using it from the bag switches it; in the bag of the one you
      control, a beam (9 tiles, ~20 degrees) the way you aim. No batteries: the sim has none, so it never runs flat
      (no battery economy invented). Found in 12% of kitchen, garage, hardware, police, station, shed, barn,
      warehouse and factory containers you search (a hash of the container: no RNG drawn).
    - Fire: a built stove while a cook job at it is worked, a heater (brazier) while it burns (cold nights), every
      burning tile in `state.fires`. There is no campfire in the sim: the brazier is the nearest thing.
    - The query: `near(state, x, y, r)` (sources lit now within r, nearest first, at most 64), `lightAt` (level and the
      strongest source), `isLit` (level >= 0.25), `roomLit`. At night a point is lit by a source within its radius
      (beams: within the cone) in the same space: the same room indoors; outdoors, open air with a clear sight line. By
      day everything is lit. Cost follows the radius (plan chunks in reach and the sparse lists in state): near() 4-12
      us, lightAt ~8 us, the same on a 256 and a 1536 world (town at night, Release, two runs each).
    - Wired where lit already mattered: Sight (night sight is day sight in a lit room: what mainsLit did), guards hit
      harder in light at night (Power.inLight: now any light), turrets and lures see what's lit (Defences.seen), and the
      night's arrivals come in the dark (Zombies.spawnPoint: a frontier tile must be unlit, besides the old LAMP_DETER
      round built lamps).
  - **Saved fields** (all null when off, so a save with no lights changed has none of them and an older save loads as
    before; no schema bump): `SimState.lightSwitches` (JsRecord<bool>, room anchor "x,y" -> on), `Item.lit` (a
    flashlight on), `Vehicle.lights` (headlights on). Fixtures (porches, lamps) are derived from the plan by hash, not
    stored, and world generation is unchanged.

    Lights without shadows cost little here (32 is ~0.2 ms GPU and ~0.3 ms CPU over none); shadows are the cost (8 or
    more add ~0.3 ms CPU render each step). Chosen: **24 real lights, 4 with shadows** (covers every source within ~35 m
    in the densest town seen; low-end machines are the target, so not 32), glows for the rest out to 320 m.
  - **Leaks.** Thin walls (12 cm) and Godot's shadow bias let lights through: an unshadowed porch light lit the inside
    wall of a dark room. Fixed two ways: a smaller bias (0.02, normal bias 1.2; 0.015 with normal bias 0.5 gave acne), and shadows go first
    to the light of the space you're in, then to lights of other spaces whose reach comes near you; one of those left
    without a shadow isn't lit at all (it could only show through a wall). After: the dark room beside the lit one shows
    only the faint fill light (`after-room-unlit.png`). Room lights light only their room in the sim; walls that fell in
    a ruin leave gaps that the drawn light goes through (as it should), the sim's doesn't.
  - **Nights.** Darker: the night ambient from (0.08, 0.09, 0.13) to (0.045, 0.05, 0.075), the fill light at your eyes
    from 0.5 to 0.22 (range 5 m). An unlit room is dim grey, readable; a lit street is orange pools between dark;
    `before-*.png` and `after-*.png` are the same views (the old values, and no lights, for "before").

  - **Design options left for the player** (not built: Sight and sneaking are unchanged).
    1. Light draws the dead: lit windows and street lamps pull wanderers or herds at night (touches Zombies' targeting,
       Noise-like "lures", Herds' drift; a flashlight beam seen from afar).
    2. Being lit makes you seen: the dead notice you further in light, less in the dark (Sight.notices / the noticing
       radius, sneaking's bonus; the HUD's stealth readout).
    3. You see further in any light, not only a lit room (Sight.sightRadius reads lightAt instead of roomLit; fog of war
       reveals lit tiles: Fog).
    4. Street lamps and porch lights deter arrivals as built lamps do (Zombies.spawnPoint's LAMP_DETER radius; balance).
    5. Batteries: a flashlight and car batteries that run down (Items wear, Vehicles; loot for spare batteries).
  - **Left.** Glows and lit-window panes are flat (no bloom). Headlights are one spot (two lamps' glows). A lit room's
    light is a ceiling point: big halls get one light. The flashlight beam is the camera's look (pitch too); the sim's
    is the aim (yaw only). Ovens (the house's decor) don't glow while you cook (the sim doesn't say which oven).
    Colony survivors never touch switches (only the one you control). Tests: LightsTest (13), TimelineTest's mains test
    rewritten for switches; self-test steps for G and X (81 checks). Check green.
