- **2026-11-11, the driver's seat.** In a car you looked out from a point at its centre with nothing round you (the car you sat in was not drawn). The player asked for a bonnet and a dashboard with at least a speedometer and a fuel gauge, realistic in what it shows.
  - **Built.**
    - **The cabins** (`art/scripts/build_cabins.py`, new: `godot/assets/cars/cabins.glb`, 4 models, 3,792 triangles): the sedan's inside (`cabin`, `cabin_v1` for the blue one) and the pickup's (`cabin_truck`: the same cab 0.55 m further forward and 0.42 m higher, a wall and a window behind the seats, the load bed): the floor, the dashboard with a binnacle, a radio, vents, heater knobs, a glovebox, the column and stalks, the console with the gear lever and handbrake, seats, door panels, pillars, the roof lining, visors, the mirrors, the scuttle and wipers, and the bonnet in the car's colour. No glass. `steeringwheel` is apart, about its hub. `lowpoly.py` has `beam` (a bar between two points) for pillars and spokes.
    - **`World3D/Cockpit.cs`:** the cabin is put with its eye point on the camera, on the car's heading eased between the sim's 32 (so it turns smoothly); the wheel leans 25 degrees and turns with the held key; the car's outside is drawn as a shadow only. `FirstPerson.Eye` is the driver's seat now (0.38 m left of the centre; `Cockpit.Seat`), not the car's centre, and the view turns with the car (`FirstPerson.CarTurn`, added to `view.yaw` each frame; getting in turns you to face forward).
    - **`World3D/GaugeCluster.cs`:** the instruments as a 2D drawing (512 x 224) in a small viewport, shown on a panel behind the wheel, with a little light of its own and more with the headlights on.
  - **What each instrument reads.** From the sim's car: the speedometer its speed (160 SUB a tick is 22.5 km/h; the dial goes to 60); the fuel gauge the tank, and the FUEL lamp under 15%; the temperature gauge its health as engine heat (a sound engine just under the middle, a wreck at H, cold when it isn't turning), and the ENGINE lamp under half health; OIL and BATT when the engine isn't turning (no fuel, or wrecked); BRAKE standing still; LIGHTS the headlights; P R N D from the speed and whether it runs. Not in the sim, worked out in the view: the tachometer (the speed through three gears, changing at 8 and 15 km/h) and the odometer (a mileage picked by the car's id plus the road since you got in).
  - **Found on the way.** Godot's importer reads a node name ending in `_wheel` as a hint and makes it a VehicleWheel3D under another name: the model is `steeringwheel` (art/README.md lists the suffixes). The wheel's hub and rim were first placed so the rim crossed the dials: the numbers in `Cockpit.cs` and `build_cabins.py` (EYE, HUB, PANEL) are worked out so the whole panel shows between the hub and the rim's top.
