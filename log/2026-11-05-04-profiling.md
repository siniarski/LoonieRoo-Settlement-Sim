- **2026-11-05, profiling (tick cost, memory, saves):** a throwaway benchmark (scratchpad zz-bench.test.ts) and a V8 profile via node:inspector of the slowest tick, replayed exactly (zz-prof.test.ts: the sim is deterministic, so "run to tick N, profile tick N+1" works).
  - Mean tick: 0.07 ms on an ordinary 128 world; 0.3 ms with 100 colonists; 0.9-1.4 ms with ~150 zombies chasing people (128 and 256 maps). 10 ticks a second at 4x speed is 40 a second, so the sim uses well under 10% of a core. Ten days of play add about 8 MB of heap.
  - Saves: 0.4 MB on 128, 1.3 MB on 256, 4.4 MB on 512; the grid is most of it (tiles as JSON), then furniture (trees). The command log stays empty without player input. Fine for now; a tile run-length encoding would cut it a lot if it matters.
  - Spikes, fixed: (1) findWater flooded the whole map with a furniture lookup per tile; a thirsty crowd on a 256 map took 145 ms in one tick. Now it looks up wells and collectors once, skips the clean search when there are none, and stops after 20000 tiles. (2) Every zombie replanning in the same tick cost 14-20 ms with 144 of them: now at most 20 plan a path per tick (PLANS_PER_TICK); the rest keep to their old path or wait a tick. Worst steady ticks are now about 10 ms with 150 zombies.
  - Left: the first tick on a big map (rooms and reachability worked out once: 25-47 ms) and chunk generation as you explore new ground (about 10 ms a chunk) are one-offs.
  - The debug panel (`) now shows tick ms (and the worst lately), sim and draw ms per frame, and the head count, for the playtest. Drawing measured 0.3 ms a frame in the (throttled) preview pane; real FPS profiling needs a human-driven browser.
  How to scale further when needed, in order: keep every per-tick system bounded (budgets like PLANS_PER_TICK); keep far things abstract (herds, dormant zombies, settlements already are); and run the sim in a Web Worker (or a thread in Godot) so a slow tick slows the game, never the frame rate. The loop already runs the ticks a frame owes, capped at 40 a frame, so a sim that can't keep up slows down rather than freezing the screen.
